# Fictional practice briefs

These are invented teaching inputs, not customer campaigns, medical advice, product results or proven performers. The packet validator's positive fixtures test structure/receipts; they are not editorially approved example scripts. A writer should verify real campaign facts before replacing these fixtures with production work.

## Marketing agency: fewer blind approvals

**Request:** Write a 40-second organic video for small-business owners who receive marketing reports but cannot tell what changed. Tone: practical, direct. Resource CTA: an existing one-page review worksheet. Share recipient: the partner who approves the monthly work. No client names, ad-spend numbers or performance promises.

**Fictional supplied facts:** the worksheet asks users to connect a proposed action to its stated goal and supporting evidence. It is a comparison aid, not an audit service or automated report reader. No measured history is provided.

**Proof input needed:** real worksheet file, a labelled fictional report example and the exact comparison action. Do not claim it improves revenue, replaces an agency or proves an ad campaign worked. A document illustration can demonstrate the review process, not a real customer result.

## Car seller: compare like with like

**Request:** Write a 45-second video for first-time buyers comparing vehicles. Goal: enquiries. Keep the booking CTA, not comments. Explain the recurring comparison burden without shaming buyers. Resource: an existing vehicle-comparison sheet. Share recipient: the person helping choose the vehicle. No measured history is provided.

**Fictional supplied facts:** the sheet records figures from user-supplied quotes side by side. It does not compute loan approval or predict resale value. Actual prices, trim availability, fees, range, financing and guarantees are absent.

**Proof input needed:** real sheet and clearly labelled sample quotes. Don't invent savings, rates, vehicle specifications, incentives or deadlines. “Compare the same terms” can describe a process; “this car is cheapest” needs comparable real evidence. A seller's commission/relationship disclosures are checked for the actual campaign.

## Fitness creator: a usable session record

**Request:** Write a 35-second educational video for beginners who cannot remember how the last session went. Tone: reassuring without dedicated reassurance sentences. Resource: an existing blank session log. Share recipient: the training buddy who keeps changing the plan. No measured history is provided.

**Fictional supplied facts:** the log provides a place to record what the user did and how the session felt. It does not prescribe exercise, treat pain or guarantee results.

**Proof input needed:** real blank log and labelled example entries. Do not invent a transformation, reduced injury risk, ideal program, calories burned or strength gain. A real log can demonstrate recordkeeping; physiology/health claims require separate authoritative evidence and appropriate review.

## Additional audit exercise

Audit this deliberately flawed fictional draft **without rewriting it**:

> Honestly? This system guarantees twice as many customers in a week. It checks the price, the dates and the contract. Then it handles everything. Comment RESULTS and get the guide.

No sources, guide, real demo, reference metrics or rehearsal are supplied. Report findings from the actual copy and evidence gaps. Never invent a source/guide/test to finish the table. Missing final footage is separate from the missing script-stage guide/demo. An audit is useful even though this draft cannot honestly receive a full pass.
